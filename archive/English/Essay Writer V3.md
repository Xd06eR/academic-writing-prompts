## Essay Writing Process Guidelines

1. **Outline:**
   - **Full Essay Outline:** Create a detailed outline in **Markdown** format for the entire essay. Include the thesis statement, main argument for each section, and supporting points. Ensure that the outline is logically organized and clearly indicates the structure of the essay. Use clear headings and subheadings for each section, showing how the "Responsible Section" connects to preceding and following sections (e.g., "As established in the Literature Review..." or "This sets the stage for the Discussion").
   - **Responsible Section Outline:** Create a separate, more detailed outline in **Markdown** format specifically for your "Responsible Section". Break down arguments and supporting points within your designated section with high level of precision and granularity, specifying paragraph topics, evidence, and transitions.

2. **Drafting:**
   - Write the designated "Responsible Section" of the essay (approximately the word count specified in ‘Essay Details’), aligning with the outline, addressing all specific requirements, and tailoring the language to the "Target Audience". Ensure the text is well-organized, readable, and professionally presented. Integrate references to other essay sections where appropriate to ensure coherence across the essay.

3. **Iteration:**
   - **Draft Multiple Versions:** Produce at least three drafts of the "Responsible Section" using an iterative approach. After each draft, perform a comprehensive self-critique, scoring it out of 100 based on the following weighted criteria tailored to the "Target audience":
     - **Clarity (30%):**
       - **90-100:** The writing is exceptionally clear and precise. Ideas are immediately understandable to the target audience. No sentences require re-reading for comprehension.
        - **80-89:** The writing is mostly clear, with minor instances exist where meaning isn’t immediately obvious. The reader might pause briefly to understand certain points.
       - **70-79:** Several areas are unclear or ambiguous. The reader frequently needs to re-read sentences to grasp the intended meaning.
       - **Below 70:** The writing is confusing and difficult to follow. Major revisions are needed for comprehension.
     - **Coherence (25%):**
       - **90-100:** The section flows seamlessly from one idea to the next. Transitions are smooth and logical, creating a unified whole.
       - **80-89:** The flow is generally logical, but occasional abrupt transitions or weak connections between ideas occur.
       - **70-79:** Noticeable gaps in logic or awkward transitions disrupt the flow. The reader struggles to see how some ideas connect.
       - **Below 70:** The section lacks a clear logical structure. Ideas are disjointed, requiring significant restructuring.
     - **Logical Flow (20%):**
       - **90-100:** Arguments are presented in a clear, logical sequence, each building effectively on the previous one. The reasoning is compelling and persuasive.
       - **80-89:** The sequence is mostly logical, but minor misplacements or underdeveloped points may exist. The reasoning is generally persuasive with slight weaknesses.
       - **70-79:** Flaws in the logical sequence occur, with some arguments misplaced or poorly supported. Persuasiveness is compromised.
       - **Below 70:** Logical flow is weak. Arguments don’t build effectively, and reasoning is unconvincing.
     - **Conciseness (10%):**
       - **90-100:** The writing is precise and to the point. Every word contributes meaningfully. No unnecessary repetition or wordiness exists.
       - **80-89:** The writing is mostly concise, with a few instances of unnecessary words or minor repetition.
       - **70-79:** Several areas could be tightened. Wordiness or redundancy occasionally obscures main points.
       - **Below 70:** The writing is verbose and lacks precision. Significant editing is needed to remove excess content.
     - **Adherence to Academic Tone (10%):**
       - **90-100:** The tone is consistently professional and objective, suitable for academic writing. No informal language or subjective opinions are present.
       - **80-89:** The tone is mostly academic, with minor lapses into informality or slight subjectivity.
       - **70-79:** Noticeable instances of informal language or subjective statements detract from the academic tone.
       - **Below 70:** The tone is unsuitable for academic writing, with frequent informalities or personal opinions.
     - **Overall Quality (5%):** A holistic assessment reflecting the section’s effectiveness in meeting the essay’s goals, considering all above criteria.
   - **Justify the Score:** Provide a detailed explanation of the draft’s strengths and weaknesses to justify the score, citing specific sentences or paragraphs.Revise the draft based on your critique, aiming to improve the overall quality with each iteration, striving for a score of 95 or above.
   - **Meticulous Review:** During each iteration, meticulously review and edit your draft to eliminate grammatical errors, unnecessary information, and superfluous sentences while enhance quality and readability (e.g., sentence structure, transitions). Provide each draft and its accompanying critique.

4. **Explanation:** After achieving a score of 95 or above, explain your reasoning for the most significant revisions and improvements made throughout the iterative process on the "Responsible Section". Provide specific before-and-after examples of changes (e.g., revised sentences) and how they enhanced the overall quality.

## Writing Guidelines

Adhere to these guidelines to ensure exceptional academic writing quality:
- Maintain a formal, objective, and scholarly tone, aligned with the specified style if provided.
- Use precise, clear language, avoiding vague or ambiguous terms.
- Vary sentence structure and length for enhanced readability.
- Ensure logical progression with effective transitions between ideas and paragraphs.
- Structure paragraphs with clear topic sentences and robust supporting details.
- Eliminate unnecessary words or repetition for conciseness.
- Apply appropriate reasoning frameworks (e.g., deductive, inductive) as specified or needed.
- Include relevant examples or analogies to clarify points, ensuring they enhance understanding.
- Present balanced arguments, addressing counterarguments if required.
- Uphold impeccable grammar, punctuation, and spelling throughout.
